# Full JeV / Holmes confidence diagnostics

These checks use existing training logs, OOF predictions and Holmes predictions. No model was retrained or tested on new labels. The procedure is in [diagnose_jev_full_holmes.py](diagnose_jev_full_holmes.py), the compact numbers in [jev_full_holmes_diagnostics.json](jev_full_holmes_diagnostics.json), and the [Holmes reliability plot](jev_full_holmes_reliability.png) is generated by [plot_jev_full_holmes_reliability.py](plot_jev_full_holmes_reliability.py).

## Same-source JeV check

The confidence training script shuffled all 48,126 JeV questions with seed 20261005. Its `mean_online_brier_last_100` was calculated **before each example's gradient update**. Averaging the 50 complete 100-example blocks at training positions **43,101–48,100** gives a 5,000-question online LoRA Brier of **0.17796**. We reconstructed those exact IDs from the saved shuffle and their OOF action predictions. A Platt model fitted only on positions **1–43,100** scored **0.16290** on those same 5,000 questions; the original Platt fit using all 48,126 labels scored 0.16289 there, but that latter number uses the test labels during fitting. The raw chosen-action probability scored 0.16346. Window accuracy was 68.96%.

Thus the confidence LoRA already trails Platt by **0.01506 Brier on same-source JeV questions**. A Holmes-only distribution shift cannot explain the entire gap. This is a *pre-update online training measure*, not the score of one fixed final checkpoint on a held-out split. Moreover, 1,297 of the 4,841 video filenames in that window had appeared in the confidence model's earlier training prefix, although each question itself had not. Treat it as a useful diagnostic, not a clean video-disjoint blind test. The Platt comparator avoids leakage from the 5,000 window by fitting only on the prefix.

## Holmes calibration and subgroups

Using ten fixed, equal-width confidence bins, Platt has **ECE 0.02257** and the confidence LoRA has **ECE 0.05847**. ECE depends on bin choices; Brier and AUC remain the primary comparisons. Platt tracks the diagonal reasonably well. LoRA compresses many predictions around 0.4–0.6, while its high-confidence bins are overconfident. The plot omits bins with fewer than 20 questions for readability; the ECE includes all 1,837 questions.

All Holmes questions have six choices, so answer-option count cannot explain the gap. For the broad first-word group `what` (1,586 questions), Platt/LoRA Brier is **0.23019 / 0.24987**. The LoRA also loses in the other first-word groups with at least 30 questions (`which`, `the`, `arrange`); this grouping is only a rough text proxy for question type. Across the 270 videos, LoRA Brier is worse on **189** and better on **81**. With only 4–11 questions per video, individual video scores are noisy; the video-cluster bootstrap in the [final report](JEV_FULL_HOLMES_REPORT.md) is the appropriate aggregate uncertainty estimate.

These observations support a same-source learning or representation problem **as well as** a possible JeV-to-Holmes transfer problem. They do not identify whether initialization from the base model, learning rate, or mismatch between half-data and full-data answer models caused it. Isolating those factors requires a controlled follow-up experiment. The full-data answer model's chosen-answer probability is comparatively well calibrated on Holmes (Brier 0.22975 versus Platt 0.22853), so Platt remains the practical confidence baseline.
